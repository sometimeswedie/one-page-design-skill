# One-page design principles (Stone Librande)

Source: "One Page Design Philosophy" (Stone Librande, creative director at EA Maxis; video https://youtu.be/E9_wLks1kAg, 52 min, transcript ends at 51:57). Timestamps are `[mm:ss]` so a claim can be checked against the talk. Paraphrased. Read this when the page feels like a poster, a dashboard, or a document in boxes.

## Why one page
- Bibles and wikis chop a design into chunks, which **hides the relationships** between parts; a hyperlink is not a real connection [09:23], [46:55]. The wiki needs constant maintenance [09:15]; the printed Bible cannot be updated [06:44].
- **Nobody reads long documents**: readers look at headlines and pictures, then at the scroll bar, and leave if it is long [10:32]. One page gets read.
- The page is also the **designer's thinking tool**: it forces understanding, forces concise design, exposes relationships when you draw the arrows, and helps solve problems [46:04]. If it cannot be made to fit, the design is probably too complex or the diagram is the wrong shape [37:42].

## Composition (the talk's own template [23:11]-[24:32])
1. **Title** = why this page exists. If you cannot fill it in, stop [23:25].
2. **Date** on every print; it is the only way to know which copy is current [23:36].
3. **Lots of white space**. Never blow up a text page into a poster [23:56].
4. **One main illustration in the middle** to draw people in, **callouts around it**, short notes and bullets [24:19]. Anchor object, place or flow; layers reveal as the reader walks closer [30:10], [30:21].
5. **Three tiers** of importance (1 glance, 2 labels, 3 detail); below about 4 pt print type "forget it" [46:31].
6. **Every mark has a reason**; no goofy decoration [22:24].
7. **Vector, sized to the paper** (bigger paper is fine), judged **as printed** not on a screen [16:06], [24:32], [22:59].

## Choose the form from the information
- **Do a flowchart first** if new to this: the loop, what the actor can do, what feedback returns [26:14].
- **Matrix, top-down**: draw the grid, pick attributes, fill it like a crossword; most of the design is then organised [33:56]-[34:41]. A table with more than 2 dimensions breaks down [36:23].
- **Storyboard with time marks** for marketing/executives [27:52]. **Space + time chart** (Minard's Napoleon): put the time/quantity element in early [28:38], [29:25].
- **Spatial map** with expected level/timing like a musical score [16:43]; **abstract-system page** (atoms of play) [18:39]; **relationship page** between modules/units [31:39].
- **The shape of the diagram is the design**: units in rock-paper-scissors corners gave 3 choices; sliders along edges gave tunable ranges [32:58]-[33:42]. A radial diagram can lie when it folds a spectrum like a fan [39:06].
- Anti-example: a deliberately tangled flowchart [27:01].

## Audience and use
- One page can serve several audiences by layering (architect's elevation vs framing plan [11:28]); the whole team can see across departments without a meeting [30:42], and a meeting then has one **common focus** [31:03].
- Pages are **printed and handed over in person** with pencils; people write on them; marks feed the next iteration; do not just email or link a file [44:08]-[45:50].
- Success signal: people ask for a copy / "I wish I could see that" [43:57], [48:15].

## Iteration and subtraction
- Start **rough, even black and white**, test in an early meeting whether people got it, then polish [49:14]. Update for days until it can go on the wall; after that changes are subtle [49:47].
- Pages stay small and grow by modules, or split into one page per module/skill/mission/interior [21:23], [45:28]. The aspiration "whole project on one page" is pursued by pushing the edges, not by shrinking type [31:12], [50:20].
- "Subtractive art": call out the most important things instead of being all-inclusive [51:20].

## What the skill adds (not in the talk)
The talk predates AI agents. The skill adds a render/critique loop, lint gates, print-size and dark/light handling, brand hooks, and AI-illustration rules so an agent can meet the principles above without a human design review of every pass. Where the skill and the talk differ, the talk's intent (relationships visible, readable on a wall, a working document) wins.
